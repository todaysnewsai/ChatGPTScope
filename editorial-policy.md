Articles About AI aims to publish useful, accurate, and clearly sourced coverage of artificial intelligence. Our guiding principles are accuracy before speed, reader value before volume, and evidence before speculation.

## Coverage

We cover AI news, product releases, model updates, research, reviews, comparisons, practical guides, and AI tools across the industry. Our scope is not limited to a single company or assistant.

## Sources and verification

For announcements about a company or product, we prioritize primary sources such as official company newsrooms, product documentation, release notes, research papers, technical reports, and official repositories. We link to sources when they help readers verify a claim or explore it further.

We distinguish an announced feature from one that is generally available. When access is limited by region, plan, waitlist, or staged rollout, we aim to say so rather than imply universal availability.

## Claims, benchmarks, and limitations

We identify company statements as company claims when independent verification is unavailable. For research and model evaluations, we consider the stated methodology, benchmark conditions, and limitations. Results from one test do not necessarily predict performance in every real-world task.

Reviews and comparisons should explain the criteria used and acknowledge meaningful trade-offs. We do not describe a product as universally best when suitability depends on a reader's needs.

## Rumors and uncertainty

Unconfirmed leaks and speculation are not presented as established fact. If uncertainty is materially relevant to an article, we make it clear and avoid overstating what is known.

## Corrections and updates

Readers can report potential errors through our [Contact page]({{ '/contact/' | relative_url }}). We review credible reports and correct factual errors when warranted. Articles may also be updated when a product, feature, price, or availability status changes; significant changes should be reflected clearly where appropriate.

## AI-assisted work

AI tools may assist with research, organization, or drafting. The publication remains responsible for reviewing claims, checking sources, and the final published text. AI-generated material is not treated as a source of factual verification on its own.
